\begin{abstract}
A Turkish word form often has several morphological analyses, and the right one depends on the sentence. Neural disambiguators for Turkish represent each candidate analysis with its root, the surface word, or both. We present \savyar{}, a disambiguator that ranks the analyses of a rule-based analyzer without seeing any open-class root. Each word is reduced to a part-of-speech token for its root, or to one of 102 lexeme tokens for closed-class words, followed by its sequence of suffixes. A Transformer encoder scores whole-sentence candidates and is trained to rank the gold sentence above sentences in which one word carries a wrong analysis. On TrMor2018, under the five-split protocol of \citet{akyurek-etal-2019-morphological}, \savyar{} reaches \TBD{acc-all}\% accuracy on all words and \TBD{acc-amb}\% on ambiguous words. The analyzer proposes the gold analysis for \TBD{coverage}\% of words, and sentence context adds \TBD{context-gain} points over scoring each word alone. We report where the root abstraction fails and what these failures say about the division of labour between analyzer and ranker.
\end{abstract}
