\paragraph{Sentence context.}
Scoring each word on its own reaches \TBD{single-amb}\% on ambiguous words, against \TBD{acc-amb}\% with beam search over the sentence (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). \citet{akyurek-etal-2019-morphological} report a similar comparison for Morse, where context raises ambiguous-token accuracy from 94.38\% to 96.21\%, and in the experiments of \citet{shen-etal-2016-role} full-sentence context raises ambiguous-token accuracy on the hand-tagged TrMor2006 test from 88.65\% to 91.03\%. Figure~\ref{fig:by-candidates} shows that the gain from context \TBD{grows/shrinks} with the number of candidates. With gold neighbours, accuracy on ambiguous words rises to \TBD{oracle-amb}\%. The gap of \TBD{oracle-gap} points to beam search is the cost of decoding with predicted rather than gold context.

\paragraph{Ambiguity profile.}
Figure~\ref{fig:candidates} shows how many analyses the analyzer proposes per word. On the held-out METU-Sabancı words, 53.1\% have a single analysis, 26.0\% have two and 3.0\% have six or more. Earlier work reports 1.60 analyses per token on the TrMor2006 training data for the analyzer of \citet{oflazer-1994-two} \citep{shen-etal-2016-role}, against 1.97 per word for \savyar{} on METU-Sabancı. The figures come from different data and count different units, so they show only that the two analyzers produce ambiguity of a similar order.
