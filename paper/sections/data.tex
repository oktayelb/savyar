\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

We work with TrMor2018 \citep{akyurek-etal-2019-morphological}, the corpus on which the most recent work on Turkish morphological disambiguation is evaluated. It holds 34,673 sentences and 460,669 tokens: 71,915 punctuation marks and 388,754 words, 280,296 of which carry at least one suffix. Its annotation follows the inflectional-group format of \citet{oflazer-1994-two}. It was disambiguated semi-automatically in several passes and disagrees with a hand-tagged subset of 26,819 words in 4.4\% of cases \citep{akyurek-etal-2019-morphological}, which bounds the accuracy any system can demonstrate on it.

\paragraph{Adapter.}
The inflectional-group format does not match the suffix inventory of \savyar{}, so an adapter maps every gold annotation to a root and a sequence of \savyar{} suffix names. It translates the features of each inflectional group into suffix names and resolves the cases in which one feature corresponds to several suffix types by comparing with the analyses that the analyzer produces for the word. Treebank lemmas that the lexicon treats as derived are expanded through a table of 28,480 dictionary decompositions, so that gold \tr{kullan-ıyor} `uses' becomes \tr{kul-la-n-ıyor}; the table rewrites gold labels only and never adds a candidate. Adaptation keeps 34,658 of the 34,673 sentences and leaves no gold annotation unmappable.

\paragraph{Splits.}
\citet{akyurek-etal-2019-morphological} give TrMor2018 no fixed split and average over five random 80/10/10 splits of its sentences. We follow them (\S\ref{sec:setup}). Each split trains on about 27,700 sentences and scores about 37,700 words. No other corpus is used, for training or for evaluation.
