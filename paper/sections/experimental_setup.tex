\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
We adapt all 34,673 TrMor2018 sentences and split them at random into 80\% training, 10\% development and 10\% test, once for each of the seeds 1 to 5, following \citet{akyurek-etal-2019-morphological}. For every split we train a freshly initialised model on the training part for eight epochs and keep the epoch with the highest accuracy on the ambiguous words of the development part. Each test sentence is processed in one forward pass (\S\ref{sec:ranker}). We report the mean and standard deviation over the five splits. No other treebank is used in these runs.

\paragraph{Scored tokens.}
TrMor2018 has 460,669 tokens: 71,915 punctuation marks and 388,754 words. Of the words, three groups are left unscored: those whose surface contains a digit (1.47\%), those the treebank leaves unanalysed or tags \tok{?} (0.98\%), and the non-final components of reduplications, tagged \tok{Dup} (0.02\%). Together these are 2.50\% of the words, against the digits and proper-noun tags masked by \citet{akyurek-etal-2019-morphological}.

Every other word is scored, pronouns included. A TrMor2018 pronoun carries a subtype feature (\tok{Pers}, \tok{Demons}, \tok{Ques}, \tok{Quant}) that classifies the lexeme rather than adding a suffix, and the ranker already sees that lexeme as a token of its own (\S\ref{sec:encoding}), so the feature maps to no suffix. Treebank lemmas that spell a pronoun together with its possessive, such as \tr{biri} `one of them' and \tr{hepsi} `all of them', are normalised to the base lexeme and the possessive suffix. These 10,596 pronoun tokens are 2.72\% of the words of the corpus, 53\% of them have more than one candidate analysis, and the analyzer reaches the gold analysis for 99.7\% of them.

Punctuation is scored but never reaches the ranker. The adapter removes it, and the analysis of a punctuation token follows from its surface form, so we count these tokens correct. Earlier TrMor2018 results are reported over all tokens, punctuation included, so we give accuracy both with and without punctuation and neither convention is hidden.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
A word is correct when the predicted analysis has the gold root and the gold suffix sequence. The comparison is between strings: the root of the predicted analysis must equal the root of the gold annotation, and the suffix names must agree in order. Analyses that share an encoding (\S\ref{sec:encoding}) therefore receive the same score without being interchangeable; the ranker breaks such a tie arbitrarily and the choice counts as correct only when its root is the gold one. We report:
\begin{itemize}
\item word accuracy over all scored words, with words whose gold analysis the analyzer does not propose counted as errors (\emph{uninfused}, following \citealp{seker-tsarfaty-2020-pointer});
\item word accuracy over words whose gold analysis is among the candidates (\emph{infused});
\item accuracy on ambiguous words (more than one candidate) and on unambiguous words;
\item sentence accuracy, the share of sentences in which every scored word is correct;
\item analyzer statistics: coverage of the gold analysis and the number of candidates per word.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Comparability.}
Our numbers remain only indicatively comparable with earlier TrMor2018 results. Counting punctuation and scoring the closed-class pronouns removes two of the differences, but a \savyar{} analysis is still coarser than an analysis in the format of \citet{oflazer-1994-two}: the root category is only noun or verb, so a noun and an adjective with the same suffixes receive the same analysis, and there is no proper-noun feature. Errors of this kind account for a large share of the errors of earlier systems \citep{yildiz-etal-2016-morphology,ozer-korkmaz-2022-transmorph}. The comparison in Table~\ref{tab:comparison} is therefore indicative.

\paragraph{Ablations and diagnostics.}
We compare the full system with the following variants:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Random choice}: the expected accuracy of picking one candidate per word uniformly at random.
\item \emph{Single word}: each word is scored on its own, as a one-word sentence, so the ranker has no context.
\item \emph{No synthetic ambiguity}: the ranker is trained on the real ambiguous words only (\S\ref{sec:training}).
\item \emph{Root-aware}: open-class roots receive their own tokens instead of \tok{N} and \tok{V}. \TBD{describe vocabulary cut-off and training setup}
\item \emph{Gold neighbours}: each ambiguous word is scored with every other word reduced to its gold analysis. This uses gold information and serves as an upper bound on what knowing the analyses of the neighbours could add.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Implementation.}
The system is implemented in Python with PyTorch. Each run trains on one NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4050 Laptop GPU with 6\,GB of memory and takes \TBD{train-time} minutes; analysing and disambiguating the test part of one split takes \TBD{decode-time} seconds.
