Table~\ref{tab:main} gives the results on TrMor2018. Over all scored words, \savyar{} is correct on \TBD{acc-all}\% of words. On words whose gold analysis the analyzer proposes, accuracy is \TBD{acc-infused}\%, so analyzer coverage accounts for \TBD{coverage-gap} points of the remaining error. Unambiguous words are correct exactly when the analyzer's single candidate is the gold one, so their accuracy, \TBD{acc-unamb}\%, measures coverage on this subset. The standard deviation across splits is at most \TBD{max-sd} points (Figure~\ref{fig:splits}).

Table~\ref{tab:comparison} places these numbers next to published results. The closest comparison is Morse, evaluated under the same protocol: \savyar{} is \TBD{delta-morse-all} points \TBD{above/below} Morse on all words and \TBD{delta-morse-amb} points \TBD{above/below} on ambiguous words. Because \savyar{} predicts a coarser analysis and does not score punctuation (\S\ref{sec:setup}), the difference bounds neither system's accuracy on the other's task.

\subsection{Held-out METU-Sabancı sentences}
\label{sec:metu}

As a second evaluation, we train one model on all five treebanks and test it on 1,000 METU-Sabancı sentences (9,177 words) that are withheld from every treebank. On these words the analyzer returns 1.97 analyses per word on average, with a maximum of 27; 46.9\% of the words have more than one analysis, and the gold analysis is among the candidates for 98.2\% of them. With beam decoding, \savyar{} reaches \TBD{metu-acc-all}\% on all words and \TBD{metu-acc-amb}\% on ambiguous words.
